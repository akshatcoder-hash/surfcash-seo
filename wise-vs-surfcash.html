Wise vs SurfCash

Wise moves money from a bank. SurfCash pays a Vietnamese QR from crypto. Same destination — dong in a local account — different jobs. Pick the one that matches how you already hold money.

They are not the same product

People search “Wise vs SurfCash” and “WisePay Vietnam vs crypto” because both end as VND in someone else’s bank. That is the only overlap. Wise is a licensed bank-to-bank transfer app. You fund it from a US, EU, UK, or similar account. The recipient gets a local transfer in one to three days, if you typed their account details correctly.

SurfCash is a non-custodial wallet. You already hold USDC or other crypto. You scan the same VietQR a Vietnamese banking app already shows. They see a normal incoming transfer in seconds. They never open either app.

Bank only, no crypto? Wise is still the straightforward path — do not buy crypto just to scan a QR once. Already living on digital dollars? Wiring through Wise every month is the long way around.

Where Wise is the right tool

Wise is good at the job it was built for. Salary in a normal bank, someone in Vietnam with a bank, a receipt, a regulated company, and no desire to touch crypto. That is a remittance. Use Wise. Same for a parent sending money home, a client paying an invoice, or a one-off deposit from abroad when the sender has never held USDC. Slower, usually more expensive than a QR scan. It still works. Wise can send to Vietnam.

What it does not do: scan the VietQR on a rent invoice, a café counter, or a motorbike shop. If your landlord only shows the QR their bank app already has, a wire is extra steps — account number, waiting, chasing the transfer.

Where SurfCash is the right tool

1. You already hold the balance as crypto

Income lands in USDC or another token. Converting to a bank, then wiring through Wise, then waiting, is two extra hops. Scan the QR. They get dong.

2. Rent, deposits, the month — not a one-off

Vietnam leases want VND in a local account, often with a printed VietQR. Recurring rent and a two-month deposit are the SurfCash loop. Full walkthrough: how to pay rent without a Vietnamese bank.

3. The recipient should not have to set anything up

Every Vietnamese banking app can show a VietQR. Family can screenshot it. A shop already has it on the counter. No new account on their side.

4. The waiting balance can earn

Parked funds earn yield — up to 15% via Perena. USD* Protected is the fixed, insured option. Yield is a user benefit, not a reason to skip checking the send fee. Wise also offers interest in some markets; that does not make it a VietQR wallet.

Fees, without the all-in trick

SurfCash’s platform fee is 0.5% per payment. Network cost, or whatever it cost you to fund the wallet, is separate. Both show before you confirm. 0.5% is not “everything you will ever pay.”

On a 12,000,000 VND (~$480) rent payment, the platform fee is about $2.40. A Wise-class wire on the same amount is often quoted around 2.5% plus whatever your sending bank stacks on. Useful if you already hold crypto. Not a reason to onboard to crypto if you only have a bank.

Other QR apps exist. Moreta is about 1.5% per payment. LocalPay is spread-based with no flat fee published. Both can receive and send. Which should you pick?

Pick Wise if the money starts in a bank, you do not hold crypto, and you are sending to a person with account details. One-off invoice, family support, a deposit from abroad — that is Wise’s job.

Pick SurfCash if you live in Vietnam (or pay here every month), you already hold digital dollars, and the other side has a VietQR. Rent, groceries, a landlord who will not take a card. They see dong. You keep the keys.

Do not use both on the same payment. Double-pay — wire from Wise and also scan a QR “just in case” — is a real failure mode. One rail, one receipt.
