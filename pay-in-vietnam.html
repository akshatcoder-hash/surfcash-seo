VietQR for foreigners: pay like a local in Vietnam

Vietnam is one of the most cashless countries in Southeast Asia — but the rails are VietQR, not Visa. Here's how a foreigner taps in without a local bank.

What is VietQR?

VietQR is the national QR standard for bank-to-bank payments in Vietnam, run on the NAPAS network. Nearly every shop, café, street vendor and landlord displays one. It's how locals pay each other instantly, at zero or near-zero cost.

The catch for foreigners: VietQR is normally accessed through a Vietnamese bank app. No local account, no VietQR — that's the wall.

How foreigners use VietQR anyway

1. Scan with a crypto wallet that speaks VietQR

SurfCash reads standard VietQR codes. You hold USDC, scan the vendor's QR, and the app settles VND into their bank account via a licensed local partner.

2. The vendor sees dong, not crypto

From their side it's identical to a local paying with a bank app. They don't need a wallet, a new app, or any idea what Solana is.

3. Works at personal and business QRs

Street vendors and small landlords usually run personal VietQR accounts. Apps built only for merchant QRs fail here — this is the #1 complaint about the first-generation travel apps. SurfCash is built for both.
