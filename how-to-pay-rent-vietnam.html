How to pay rent in Vietnam without a bank account

You earn in USD or crypto. Your landlord wants VND in their bank. Here's the exact problem and the cleanest fix in 2026.

The problem

Most Vietnamese landlords want rent paid into a local bank account via bank transfer or VietQR. As a foreigner on a tourist visa, a digital nomad, or a remote worker without a work permit, opening a Vietnamese bank account is painful or impossible. Your Visa/Mastercard can't do a local bank transfer to a landlord. ATMs cap withdrawals and charge $5–$7 each.

So people end up paying cash (480 bills a month, anyone?), wiring through Wise and eating a 2.5% fee and a 1–2 day wait, or using a Telegram "money guy" at 10%.

The fix: scan the landlord's VietQR with crypto

Step 1 — Get a SurfCash account

Download the app. Import your existing wallet or buy USDC in-app. Non-custodial: your keys, your money.

Step 2 — Ask the landlord for their VietQR

Almost every Vietnamese landlord has one — it's the same QR their bank app shows for receiving transfers. Your rent invoice usually has it printed on it.

Step 3 — Scan and send

Scan the QR, enter the rent amount in VND. SurfCash converts your crypto and sends dong directly into the landlord's bank account.

Step 4 — Show the receipt

The landlord sees the money in their banking app, usually in under 30 seconds. They don't need to know anything about crypto.

SurfCash charges 0.5% per payment. On a 12,000,000 VND (~$480) monthly rent, that's about $2.40 — versus ~$12.50 in Wise fees, or a $7 ATM run, or losing 10% to an informal channel.

Fee is the SurfCash platform fee; your crypto network or funding-source cost is separate and shown before you confirm.

Deposits, too

Vietnam leases usually want 1–2 months' deposit. Send it the same way, and keep the receipt — you'll want it when the lease ends. Because SurfCash is non-custodial and settled on-chain, you get a permanent record of every payment. No more "did I actually pay two deposits?" arguments.
