Pay a Vietnam landlord with crypto

They do not want crypto. They want VND in a Vietnamese bank, on the 1st, with a name they recognise. Crypto is how you fund the payment — not what they receive.

What the landlord actually needs

A typical HCMC or Hanoi lease is denominated in dong (sometimes quoted in USD, always settled in VND). The owner or the building accountant already has a bank app. They already generate a VietQR. They will not open Binance, they will not take a SWIFT, and they will not learn what a wallet is.

So the job is not “send USDT to a landlord.” The job is: convert the balance you already hold, land VND in their account, keep a receipt. Same rail locals use. They never see crypto. How VietQR works for foreigners.

What to ask them for

Ask in English or Vietnamese. You need three things, not a lecture:

1. Their VietQR

Every Vietnamese banking app can show one. Many leases print it. A screenshot of the QR is enough. Confirm the account name matches the person on the contract — agencies sometimes collect into a company account.

2. The amount in VND

If rent was quoted in USD, lock the dong figure in writing for that month. FX drift is how “$800” becomes an argument. Pay the VND number they named, not a USD guess.

3. The transfer content

Vietnamese banks show a short memo. Use your name + unit + month (e.g. “ASH 12A rent Sep”). They match it in their statement. Do not leave it blank.

Rent, deposit, utilities — three different QRs sometimes

Monthly rent is usually one QR, one account. The 1–2 month deposit often goes to the same account; sometimes the building or agency uses a different one. Electricity, water, and internet are frequently a third QR — the building’s, not the owner’s. Scan the QR they send for that bill. Do not reuse last month’s rent QR for EVN.

Pay the deposit the same way as rent and keep the receipt. When you leave, the fight is rarely “did crypto work.” It is “did you pay two months, and into which account.” A bank-side incoming transfer with your memo is the record they already understand. What they see on their end

A normal incoming transfer. Dong. Their bank’s app. Seconds, not days. They do not install anything. They do not approve a crypto withdrawal. If you use SurfCash, you scan their VietQR, enter the VND amount, confirm, and they get a standard credit.

Show them the receipt screenshot if they ask. Most landlords care that the money is there before they hand over keys or renew. They do not need a product pitch.

What not to do

Do not send USDT to their exchange account because a Facebook group said landlords “take crypto now.” Most do not. The ones who do are taking on a job they did not ask for, and you have no lease-shaped receipt.

Do not use a Telegram money guy for a deposit. Fast, expensive, no useful record when the lease ends.

Do not wire from Wise and also scan a QR “just in case.” Double-pay is a real failure mode. One rail, one receipt.

Do not pay USD cash if the contract is in VND and you will need the deposit back in VND. Mix the currency and you donate the spread twice.

If they refuse anything but cash

Some older owners still want a stack of bills on the 1st. That is their right. Cash works; it is just a bad record. Ask once for the VietQR (“same as transferring from a Vietnamese bank”). If they still want cash, ATM it and get a handwritten receipt with the amount in VND and both names. Do not argue product at the door.

If they agree to a transfer — most city landlords already do — pay from the crypto you already hold. They get dong. You keep the receipt.
